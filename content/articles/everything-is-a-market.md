Everything is a market (and you are crossing the bid-ask spread)!


...well what is a market in the first place?
It is a place where you can exchange goods, usually for money.
The key event that a market facilitates is a trade, which is when two parties agree to exchange a good for money.
When a trade happens, there are two key people: takers and makers.
The maker is the person who holds out the sign, makes the post online, or is selling a good.
They wait with a posted price until their good is sold.
The taker on the other hand, comes in, thinks the price is a sensible deal, and then buys the good for the listed price.

_Currency exchanges are market makers_


For every good, there is a fair price, which is what the good is actually worth.
If you buy it or produce the good for less than the fair price you make money.
If you sell it for above the fair price you also make money.
So the maker will always sell the good for a bit above the fair price or buy the good for a bit below the fair price, while the taker pays an extra fee on top of or below the fair.


Every time you buy and sell a good, service, contract or anything else, it is a market.
Markets are very different from good to good, but the maker and taker dynamics are a common thread.
Which would you rather be? A maker or a taker?
Well obviously the maker is the one that is actually making money, but they also have to stand around selling the good all day.
A taker pays for convenience - they can buy the good any time the maker is open for business.
